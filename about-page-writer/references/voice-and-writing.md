# Voice and writing

## Keep the site's voice

The page must sound like the rest of the website and like the owner. Before writing, capture:

- **Language(s) and mix** — e.g. English headings with Romanian text; keep it if it's part of the brand.
- **Narrator and address** — we / I / the business name; *you* formal or informal (*tu* / *dumneavoastră*).
- **Rhythm** — sentence length, short punchy lines or long explanations, capitalised headings, lists.
- **Vocabulary** — the exact words they use for their work, services and customers ("pacienți", "clienți", "content", "brand"). Reuse them; don't swap in synonyms.
- **Tone** — warm, technical, playful, formal, confident.
- **Signature phrases** — lines worth keeping word for word.
- **Weak spots** — vague or empty passages to improve.

Sources, in order: the owner's explicit wishes > how the owner writes in chat > the site's best pages (often homepage and services, not an old About page) > a plain default.

## How to edit

- **Keep good original text word for word.** Change a passage only to make it clearer, more accurate, more specific, or to answer a missing brand question.
- **Add new text in the same voice:** same narrator, register, sentence length and vocabulary as the surrounding copy.
- **Fix, don't flatten:** when a sentence makes an unsupported claim, narrow the claim and keep the rest of the sentence's style.
- **Keep the business's own names** for services and sections unless the owner changes them.
- If the site's own copy is generic, use the owner's way of talking and the site's best passages as the model, and tell the owner.
- Show the voice in the diagnosis with 2–3 observations and the revised passages, so the owner can say "more formal" or "more relaxed" before the final version.

## What a great About page does

- **Opens with who they are**, plainly: name, what, for whom, where.
- **Is about the customer as much as the business:** what problem they solve, for whom, and how working together feels.
- **Shows real people** — names, roles, a photo suggestion — not "a dedicated team".
- **Puts proof next to each claim**: a number, a client (with permission), a review theme, a credential.
- **Answers the decision questions**: who it's for and not for, how it works, how pricing is approached, what's different.
- **Tells one true story** if there is one (why they started, a turning point) — short and specific. Never invent one.
- **Ends with one clear next step** using the real link, phone or email.
- **Is as long as the substance allows** — a strong 300-word page beats a padded 900-word one.

## Avoid

- Stock phrases that could fit any business: passionate about, innovative solutions, seamless, cutting-edge, one-stop shop, your trusted partner, dedicated team of professionals, in today's fast-paced world, we pride ourselves. Keep a phrase only if the owner really uses it.
- Reflex lists of three, "not just X, but Y", moral closing lines, stacked adjectives, heavy em-dash use.
- Test for the opening and the "what's different" lines: *could a competitor paste this unchanged?* If yes, add the specific detail.
